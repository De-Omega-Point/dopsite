The learning pathway

Each module connects concepts to real-world practice, applied industry examples, a portfolio artefact and a competency check. The curriculum below is an approved internal direction shared publicly as an overview, not an open enrolment offer.

01

Human dignity, agency, consent and practical benefit

Transfer: Care, education, public services
02

AI fundamentals

Models, capabilities, limits and reliable use

Transfer: Operations, research, digital products
03

AI risk and threat literacy

Failure modes, misuse, incidents and escalation

Transfer: Cybersecurity, governance, procurement
04

Responsible AI governance

Accountability, oversight and revocable authority

Transfer: Leadership, compliance, policy
05

Founder / Strategic leadership

Learn it. Build it. Govern it.

The founder stream prioritises system maps, high-impact decisions, practical builds, resource allocation and verification gates over passive classroom instruction.

01 / SeeVisualise the system and human need
02 / DoRun a guided practical lab
03 / DecideMake and document a governance call
04 / ProveReview evidence and transfer the skill
Competency-based

Evidence that travels with you

Participants build a portfolio of risk maps, prototype workflows, evaluation plans, governance decisions and a final capstone. These capabilities can transfer into technology, education, healthcare, public service, retail, professional services and emerging industries.

Completion criteria, delivery dates, assessment rules and any formal credentials will be published only after they are defined and verified.

Human agency comes first. People must be able to understand, challenge, redirect and stop consequential AI actions. Learning outcomes are tested against this principle, not just model performance.
