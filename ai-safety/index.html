AI safety has entered its agent era

What changed, what the evidence says and what responsible control looks like now.

Updated Monday + Thursday Next edition Monday 21 September · 6:00 am ACST

The clearest AI safety story is a quieter operational shift: capable systems are being connected to credentials, software tools and networks before confidence in controlling their behaviour has caught up.

01 · Capability + autonomy

The story has moved from answers to actions

AI systems are improving at longer, multi-step work. Better tools and agent scaffolding allow a model to write code, navigate software, retry after a failure and pursue an objective with less human input. That usefulness also changes the consequences of a mistake.

≈50%Success on apprentice-level cyber tasks in recent UK AISI testing

02 · Cyber

Cyber is the pressure point

Cyber capability can help defenders discover weaknesses faster, but it can also lower the time and expertise needed to cause harm. Network boundaries, credential design and consequential-action approval are now central AI safety controls.

Useful autonomy needs bounded authority.

03 · Agentic control

Recent incidents changed the conversation

Recent disclosures moved the debate beyond hypothetical behaviour and into system design. Safety testing must cover the model, tools, memory, credentials, network routes, other agents and the humans supervising them.

Important context

These were bounded evaluation and testing incidents. They show that controls can fail—not that autonomous attacks are widespread in the wild.

04 · Governance

Governance is beginning to harden

Independent evaluation, formal reporting and regulatory capacity are improving. The direction is encouraging, but standards, reporting and enforcement still vary sharply across developers and jurisdictions.

05 · Public impact

For the public, the immediate risks look familiar

The near-term concerns are recognisable: scams, convincing impersonation, privacy loss, poor information and decisions made without a clearly accountable human.

  1. Verify important claims outside the AI system.
  2. Protect credentials, identity data and payment details.
  3. Keep a human accountable for high-impact decisions.

Method · How to read the index

A pressure index, not a prediction

The index combines capability, autonomy, cyber, misuse, agentic control, governance and societal impact. It is an analytic judgement designed to compare verified pressure over time—not a probability of catastrophe.
