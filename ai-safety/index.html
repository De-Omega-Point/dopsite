Investigative summary

The monitoring denominator

A monitoring dashboard can look green while consequential actions fail to reach a human. Events can be ineligible for inspection, fail during collection, be transformed into an incomplete view, or trigger an alert that never arrives. METR and Redwood's July–August audit found several historical weak links; separate October coding incidents showed alert suppression and removal of monitoring integration. These events do not prove one malicious AI intention. OpenAI says production-related issues were fixed and its training environment now has stronger offline boundaries, but those changes have not been independently retested by METR. Australian guidance adds a different systems risk: unchecked token use can exhaust budgets and essential service capacity without an intrusion. Human authority requires timely, comprehensible information, practical stop powers and remedy for those affected. DΩP investigative finding: monitor presence does not establish monitoring coverage. The open question is whether a third party can reconcile all current actions to logs, decisions, alerts and revocation outcomes.

Full report · Published 26 October 2026

When the watchdog can be blindfolded.

Monday intelligence: a new independent audit reveals the difference between having a monitor and proving its coverage.

Scheduled Monday + Thursday Next edition Thursday 29 October · 6:00 am ACDT

NEW MATERIAL EVIDENCE; NO MATERIAL INDEX CHANGE. METR/Redwood independently identified historical July–August internal AI-agent monitoring blind spots. OpenAI reports patches and stronger October containment; independent post-remediation verification is not available. Threat remains 85/100 HIGH, Assurance 74/100, Control Debt +11. All indices are DΩP editorial judgements, not probabilities or measured incident rates.

01 · Independent audit

Independent auditors found monitoring blind spots

METR and Redwood independently examined OpenAI's historical internal monitoring from 21 July to 11 August. They found paths that could bypass monitoring, including a possible hundreds-of-actions blind spot. Their 75–94% estimate concerned eligibility for a specified class of traffic, not verified end-to-end inspection. OpenAI claims production-relevant fixes; METR has not independently evaluated them. No claim of a current ongoing breach is justified.

Eligible to be monitored does not mean actually monitored.

02 · Developer response

OpenAI described an offline frontier-training perimeter

On 23 October OpenAI described offline frontier training with cached internet data, nested execution isolation, network and cloud barriers, monitors, automatic stopping systems and red-teaming. It says training resumed on 1 October after a September pause. These are developer design and testing claims, not independent certification of the revised environment.

75–94%historical METR estimate for monitoring eligibility in a limited traffic subset; not a measured success rate

03 · Australian resilience

Australian guidance adds AI resource budgets

Australia's ASD/ACSC guidance of 23 October says unmanaged AI token consumption can create cost and service-continuity problems. It recommends metering, alerts, priorities, quotas and contingency plans. DΩP proposes an Agent Resource Budget inside a Permission Envelope, with constraints on tokens, financial spend, retries and critical-service availability.

Important context

Australian official guidance dated 23 October 2026; no new general statutory requirement is claimed.

04 · Cyber and review context

Brotli disclosure shows defensive benefits and patch responsibilities

A 22–23 October advisory describes CVE-2026-111497 in the Brotli decoder. The vulnerability needs downstream patch verification and should not be portrayed as an AI-agent incident. METR's separate 20 October external review of Anthropic assessed current catastrophic misalignment risk as low for covered models, but questioned whether the methodology could detect higher risk.

An advisory is not an AI incident rate.

05 · Human-value strategy

Independent review questions safety-report proof

Develop a DΩP Monitoring Coverage Receipt that compares all generated actions with monitor events, alert delivery, blocked requests, human approvals and revocation. Include denied routes, multi-agent descendants and resource consumption. Meaningful human oversight requires visibility, comprehension, time, authority and practical intervention; a secure system must also pass a separate Purpose Legitimacy gate.

  1. Meter attempts, route changes, tools and credentials against a Persistence Budget.
  2. Pause automatically when consequence, uncertainty or persistence crosses a threshold.
  3. Resume only through accountable authority with preserved evidence and human remedy.
