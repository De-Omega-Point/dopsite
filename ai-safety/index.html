Key developments and continuity watchpoints

The FAA reported no indication of a cyberattack

The FAA says instability affected 17 of 22 en-route control centres before its 13 November nationwide ground stop. The agency reported that some radio and radar functions remained available and flights already airborne landed safely. In its 15 November account, it reported no indication of cyberattack and said the root cause was still under investigation. That day’s cancellation total is not a count of flights cancelled because of the ground stop. The case demonstrates a decision to pause on safety grounds, not proof the fallback worked equally well for everyone.

Read more One investigation does not establish prevalence

On 20 November, Amazon Threat Intelligence reported observing autonomous AI agents used in a September intrusion campaign. This is a first-party account of a specific investigation, not a global incident count or independent proof of actor identity. The operational lesson is to limit tool and data access, monitor actions outside scope and rehearse how an authorised person stops a run.

Read more A compromised AI dependency is not evidence of AI authorship

GitHub’s malware advisory identifies two malicious agent-framework releases, 1.26.1 and 1.26.2, which it says were removed from PyPI. The advisory does not establish how many installations were affected or whether an AI system authored the malware. If exposure is suspected, use the organisation’s incident-response process to isolate and rebuild affected environments, review accessible credentials and rotate them as appropriate.

Read more A software update is not proof of a safe outcome

The 6 November NHTSA recall query examines the adequacy of an earlier Waymo school-bus remedy and post-remedy reports. This is a continuing watchpoint, not a new filing this week or a final finding. It does not establish a population-wide incident rate or that a subsequent remedy works. Request evidence for the deployed configuration in representative conditions, including the experience of people who did not choose to be near an automated vehicle.

Australian cyber guidance recommends containing AI agents during testing, monitoring activity and keeping human oversight rather than relying only on prompts. For a critical workflow, identify the person who can pause the system, the usable fallback and the route for people to correct an error.
