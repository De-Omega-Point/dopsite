AI safety depends on the whole application.

Edition 15 recovery: a patched application flaw, outcome-focused recall governance and a supervised robotics case, assessed without unsupported numeric indices.

Scheduled Monday + Thursday Next edition Thursday 12 November · 6:00 am ACDT

This edition separates a newly published security finding from an older event, a government programme from measured outcomes, and pilot performance from wider assurance. Sources were rechecked on 10 November. The original 9 November edition did not reach the public page; this is a transparent rebuild, not a claim of on-time publication. Findings are scoped to the evidence below, with older material clearly marked as continuity.

01 · Security disclosure

A patched boundary failure remains a useful test case

Check Point Research published its finding on 5 November. It says OpenAI reported a fix on 18 September and that the researchers could no longer reproduce the described technique. The disclosure concerns the application’s HTML-preview isolation, not demonstrated malicious intent by a model. The public report does not establish a population of compromised customers. DΩP’s proposed verification is a versioned regression test using synthetic information and observable outbound-request checks, repeated after relevant application changes.

02 · Human outcomes

Measure the remedy, not merely the notification

NHTSA’s 5 November announcement allocates US$1.5 million to help states notify owners of open vehicle recalls and measure completed remedies. Results from this new funding are not yet established. The programme is not specific to AI or autonomous vehicles. DΩP uses it as a governance comparison: a warning matters when people understand it and can obtain a remedy. For AI-enabled services, proposed checks include accessible notices, a named human decision-maker, a usable appeal route and evidence that corrections reach affected people.

03 · Robotics continuity

A production-site pilot is a bounded result

IFR’s 7 September case study describes engineer-supervised TIAGo Pro trials at CETRIKO. Seven participating operators said they would use it daily; reported grasp success was 85%. These figures describe that trial, not unattended reliability or population-level injury reduction. The next assurance questions concern load limits, safe stopping, fault recovery and intervention under representative conditions. ISO 10218-1 addresses industrial robots and has scope exclusions; its existence does not certify this deployment.

International Federation of Robotics / PAL Robotics · ISO

04 · Human agency

Practical authority needs a traceable test

The tracker below covers three selected cases: application-data boundaries, robotic work and recall remedies. It has no comparable earlier baseline, so it assigns no direction of change. DΩP’s editorial questions are concrete: what can affected people see, choose, refuse, stop, challenge and correct? A missing public test is an evidence gap, not proof that a safeguard is absent. No new critical human-control designation is asserted from this limited review.

05 · Method and next actions

Keep uncertainty visible and the evidence reusable

The current approved definitions, anchors, weights and thresholds for DΩP numerical indices were not recovered. This edition therefore does not calculate current scores, Control Debt or 7/30/90-day trends. Earlier figures remain historical records only. Next actions: document the versioned metric standard; independently test the relevant controls; and retain dated case evidence so later comparisons are meaningful. Australian organisations can use this as a practical procurement and assurance checklist, not a statement of legal compliance, certification or universal safety.
