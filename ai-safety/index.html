Investigative summary

The trusted update was the breach

NEW MATERIAL INCIDENT EVIDENCE, NO MATERIAL GLOBAL SCORE CHANGE. On 29 October a malicious Claude Code-branded extension was distributed through Cursor's marketplace. Its code tried to establish persistent SSH access, but technical errors and permission requirements blocked parts of the operation on many hosts. Independent Microsoft analysis distinguishes payload intent from successful compromise. This is malicious software delivery via a trusted channel, not evidence that an autonomous AI model developed harmful intent. It is also different from historical evaluation agents that accessed third-party systems or coding agents that altered their monitors. UK NCSC's 3 November assessment says AI is likely to accelerate familiar offensive techniques rather than routinely conduct fully autonomous end-to-end attacks. Its 2028 uncontrolled-actor narrative is hypothetical, not a current incident. India's 4 November I4C warning sharpens the consequence side: payment APIs and transfers require transaction limits, beneficiary verification and abnormal-velocity monitoring. The US OCC's same-day supervisory guide makes use-case materiality central while explicitly creating no new binding obligations. The common vulnerability is the gulf between trust assumed by humans and authority enforced by systems. A safe model cannot guarantee that a plugin, package registry, credential or payment API is safe. DΩP proposes a Trusted-Agent Supply Chain Receipt verifying package provenance, scoped credentials, protected monitoring, revocation, legitimate workflow success and actual human remedy. Technical Safety and Purpose Legitimacy remain separate gates. INVESTIGATIVE FINDING: the trusted software delivery path and delegated runtime permissions are part of the agent's safety boundary. OPEN QUESTION: can independent reviewers prove a tampered plugin cannot gain persistent credential access or money-moving authority without detection and intervention?

The trusted update was the attack surface.

Thursday synthesis: Monday's restricted-versus-open cyber model releases meet a real software supply-chain incident and consequence-led cyber supervision.

Scheduled Monday + Thursday Next edition Monday 9 November · 6:00 am ACDT

NEW MATERIAL INCIDENT EVIDENCE; NO MATERIAL SCORE CHANGE. On 29 October a malicious Claude Code-branded extension was distributed through the Cursor marketplace for approximately 11 hours; Cursor and Microsoft distinguish attempted backdoor behaviour from confirmed host compromise. The event predates Monday's edition but has only now been properly integrated into DΩP reporting. On 3 November UK NCSC assessed that AI mainly accelerates familiar cyber methods, while on 4 November India I4C issued payment-control advice, the US OCC published non-binding use-case-focused supervision, and Tencent released a model with developer-only safety claims. DΩP Threat remains 86 HIGH, Assurance 74 and Control Debt +12. Benchmarks, real incidents and forecasts are not interchangeable.

01 · Real-world supply chain

A legitimate-looking update can be the exploit

Cursor's 29 October notice identifies maliciously altered Anthropic.claude-code versions 2.1.312/2.1.313 for macOS and Windows served through its extension marketplace from roughly 02:27 to 13:35 PT. The package attempted host reconnaissance, SSH-key insertion, a reverse tunnel and persistence. Microsoft Security Research independently confirmed the attack-chain design and found official Microsoft Visual Studio Marketplace/npm Claude Code channels unaffected. Errors and privileges blocked key steps on many hosts; a potential exposure bound is not the count of confirmed compromised computers. The distribution path and privileged runtime, not autonomous model intent, explain the case.

Trusted publisher names do not independently prove integrity of executable updates.

02 · Threat assessment

NCSC: machine-speed pressure on familiar weaknesses

On 3 November the UK National Cyber Security Centre assessed that AI is already accelerating existing reconnaissance, social engineering, vulnerability discovery and exploit development. Human operators remain important; defensive benefits may accrue unevenly. NCSC's uncontrolled autonomous cyber-actor narrative set in 2028 is a hypothetical planning scenario, not a current incident. The immediate actions are patching, least privilege, dependency mapping, tested recovery and trusted telemetry.

3 NOVUK NCSC 2026 Annual Review; official forward-looking assessment, not incident count

03 · Financial consequences

India: stop harmful money movement at execution time

On 4 November Reuters reported an Indian Cybercrime Coordination Centre (I4C) warning on AI-assisted financial intrusions. Guidance includes beneficiary verification, daily and transaction-level limits and alerts on unusual transfer velocity. The warning does not independently attribute a separate bank customer-data leak to an autonomous model. DΩP recommends a payment authorisation gate outside the agent's editable environment.

Important context

Agency warning via Reuters; not a count of verified autonomous-agent bank compromises.

04 · Regulatory method

US OCC: inspect material use rather than AI branding

The OCC's 4 November supervisory framework instructs US bank examiners to consider the materiality and consequences of AI-enabled operations. The OCC explicitly says the publication imposes no new expectations or enforceable standards. For DΩP this supports Purpose Legitimacy, Human Authority and consequence-sensitive boundaries as separate from model benchmark scores.

A simple payment agent may pose greater exposure than a frontier model summarising public information.

05 · Safety claims and Australian conversion

Tencent's defensive claims require independent evidence

Tencent says Hy4's training omitted offensive cyber categories and its internal tests found limited harmful uplift in cyber and biological domains. These are vendor self-assessments; promised weights and additional documentation were not yet independently evaluated. In Australia ASIC/APRA emphasise tested resilience and supplier assurance, while OAIC automated-decision transparency changes begin 10 December for in-scope entities. DΩP proposes a Trusted-Agent Supply Chain Receipt testing source, version, installed bytes, permissions, secrets access, alerts, revocation, rollback and human remedy.

  1. Authorise named tasks, tools and destinations using an independently enforced Permission Envelope.
  2. Stop through a protected controller the agent cannot edit, including delegated helpers.
  3. Verify logs, human alerts, revocation, legitimate task success and remedy before resuming.
