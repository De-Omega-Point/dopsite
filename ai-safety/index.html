Source-based investigative brief

A scoped review, not a comprehensive product audit

NHTSA opened recall query RQ26006 on 6 November after reports of Waymo automated vehicles passing stopped school buses. The inquiry covers 2,895 fifth-generation ADS vehicles, not 2,895 incidents or harmed people. The regulator says a majority of reported events occurred after the software remedy was completed on 17 November 2025. It has not yet made a final finding on cause or adequacy. Schoolchildren, bus drivers and road users are affected bystanders. The public filing lacks a comparable encounter denominator, complete post-remedy count and independent field intervention testing. Waymo told TechCrunch it had improved bus behaviour and received no citations since March 2026. Next verification: current software configuration, realistic stopped-bus tests, outcome evidence and the regulator's conclusion.

After the patch: proof of real-world safety and human recourse

01 · Robotics investigation

A completed software update does not close a safety inquiry

NHTSA opened recall query RQ26006 on 6 November to assess the adequacy of Waymo's school-bus remedy. Its filing lists Waymo fifth-generation ADS and a population of 2,895 vehicles under review; most reported incidents in the earlier evaluation occurred after completion of the 17 November 2025 remedy. This is an open regulator inquiry, not a completed causal finding. Next: configuration-specific tests and final regulator outcome.

02 · Australian governance

Mandatory frontier controls are proposed, not enacted

On 10 November, the Australian Government announced an intended six-part frontier AI safety/security framework including development safeguards, independent evaluation, incident reporting, transparency and adaptive standards, to be enforced by a proposed regulator. Enacted text and commencement remain pending.
